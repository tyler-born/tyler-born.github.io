HALE: Nosecone CFD-to-FEA Verification

I ran a supersonic CFD simulation in ANSYS Fluent to get the pressure load on the nosecone, then applied that load in a static structural FEA of a 3D printed section. The material properties were deliberately conservative, so a pass here should hold up in flight.

Peak von Mises stress was about 1.2 MPa, and the tip deflected about 0.1 mm at most. The factor of safety stayed above 15 everywhere on the part.

HALE: Canard Moment Analysis

For a research active control project, I ran supersonic CFD on a canard at its maximum deflection and top speed to find the largest aerodynamic moment on it.

The maximum moment about the servo axis was 0.054 N·m at 5° deflection and Mach 1.1. The canard has a 50 mm base chord.

HALE: Carbon Fiber Tube Cutting Jig

A jig built from a repurposed 3D printer frame for cutting carbon fiber tube.

Rollers that rotate lock one axis of rotation depending on the operation, and a roller on the Z-axis bar clamps and rotates the tube. A mounting arm holds a Dremel that cuts radially or travels along the tube’s length, so the jig makes slots as well as full circumferential cuts.

Rotating the rollers and the Dremel arm by 90° gives the second configuration.

5-Arm Linkage Robotic Pen Plotter

A robotic pen plotter built around a 5-arm linkage, with two stepper motors driving the arms and a servo lifting the pen.

I draw in Inkscape and export the SVG as G-code. A Python parser reads the G-code and uses gradient descent to convert each Cartesian point into the two motor angles, minimizing the difference between the target position and the position the linkage reaches.

A linkage like this can reach the same point with more than one set of angles. The first guess is the middle of the drawing area, which finds the correct solution, and every later guess starts from the previous result, so the solver lands on the nearest solution, which is the correct one. The parser also subdivides long moves, so the pen travels in straight lines even though the kinematics are non-linear.

Belt tension is built into the design, and a 3:1 pulley ratio increases the torque at the arms.

PCB CNC Machine

A CNC machine built on an Ender 3 frame for isolation routing copper-clad PCBs and cutting acrylic and other plastics.

It runs a custom Marlin configuration with contact-based Z probing and bilinear bed leveling. I tuned spindle speed and feed rate for clean isolation cuts, and rewired the Z endstop so it levels automatically on conductive material. The flex shaft spindle sits on a PETG-CF printed bracket and runs through a 1500 W inline AC speed controller.

Smart Plug Controller

A battery-powered wall button that toggles smart plugs. It runs on an ESP32-C3 with four mechanical switches. The board stays in deep sleep and wakes only on a button press to send an HTTP request to the target plug, so a charge lasts weeks.

I milled the PCB on the machine above, and the back panel is machined acrylic cut on the same machine.

Competition Model Rocket

A model rocket for a team competition, designed for apogee accuracy, flight stability, and recovery success. Our team won.

It flew on a D12-5 engine, with a 3D printed nosecone and fins and an Estes parachute recovery system. The custom payload carries an IMU that measures acceleration, angular velocity, orientation, and temperature, in a PETG-CF housing.

I did most of the 3D design, soldered the payload electronics, and printed most of the parts.

3D Printed Mechanical Clock

A weight-driven mechanical clock with a Graham escapement. I calculated the gear ratios and escapement dynamics by hand, and it keeps time to within ±3 minutes per day.

The structure is 3D printed, and the hardware is bearings, screws, and nuts.

Competition Drone with Electromagnet Claw

A drone for a high school competition, with a servo-actuated claw. The three claw arms retract back and up to expose an electromagnet at the drone’s center, so one vehicle can grip a payload mechanically or lift ferrous payloads magnetically.

I designed all of it myself.

Fold N Hold

A fly fishing product I designed that holds a fully rigged rod folded to half length for transport. It is 3D printed in carbon fiber reinforced PETG, fits 3 to 7 weight rods, and comes with interchangeable tips in three sizes.

It sells profitably, and I have filed a provisional patent application. 25% of profits go to fly-fishing nonprofits and fisheries.

Ergo Split Keyboard

I was commissioned to design an ergonomic split keyboard with wrist rests and a numpad in the middle. The left half, numpad, and right half connect through magnetic snap wire connections, so each section can be repositioned on its own.
